INTEL'S JOURNEY

Decades of Innovation

From the founding of Intel in 1968 to ambitious sustainability commitments for the future, explore some of the moments that shaped Intel's technology and environmental journey.

1968

01

1968

Intel Founded

A new era of technology begins.

Robert Noyce and Gordon Moore rename the newly formed company NM Electronics to Intel Corporation, laying the foundation for decades of technological innovation.

1971

02

1971

First Microprocessor

Computing changes forever.

Intel debuts the 4004, the world's first commercial microprocessor, igniting the microprocessor revolution and propelling the future of computing devices.

1978

03

1978

8086 Processor

The x86 architecture arrives.

Launch of the 8086 processor establishes the x86 architecture that drives countless PCs and servers in the modern era.

1985

04

1985

386 Processor

Personal computing grows stronger.

Intel introduces the 386 processor with 32-bit architecture, ushering in a new era of performance and multitasking for personal computers.

2006

05

2006

Peak GHG Emissions

A turning point for environmental action.

This year marks Intel's highest annual greenhouse gas emissions for operations. Intel subsequently invests heavily in chemical abatement, renewable energy and energy-efficient manufacturing.

2020

06

2020

RISE Strategy

Responsibility meets innovation.

Intel launches its RISE — Responsible, Inclusive, Sustainable and Enabling — strategy and 2030 goals, targeting climate action, water stewardship and waste reduction.

2022

07

2022

Net-Zero by 2040

An ambitious climate commitment.

Intel announces its commitment to achieve net-zero greenhouse gas emissions for Scope 1 and Scope 2 across its global operations by 2040.

2023

08

2023

Renewable Electricity

Powering progress more responsibly.

Intel achieves 99% renewable electricity usage worldwide, helping lower carbon emissions and advancing the company's long-term sustainability goals.

2024

09

2024

Sustainability Summit

Collaboration for the next generation.

Intel hosts its first Sustainability Summit, bringing suppliers, government officials and industry leaders together to collaborate on sustainable semiconductor manufacturing.
